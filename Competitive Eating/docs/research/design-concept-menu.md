# Design Concept Menu — Competitive Eating

*A browseable menu of distinct creative directions for the competitive eating game. Written May 2026, for Antoine. Not opinionated — for that, see [`gameplay-design-deep-dive.md`](./gameplay-design-deep-dive.md). This doc is a buffet: 12 concepts spanning grounded to surreal, designed to be picked from, blended, or used to spark a fresh direction.*

> **What this doc IS:** A range of distinct aesthetic / world / theme directions. Each concept is profiled with a one-line pitch, visual identity, gameplay framing, ride re-themes for the rides you've already built, NPC vibes, monetization angle, audience comparisons (real Roblox games), build risk, and a sketch of what spawning in feels like.
>
> **What this doc is NOT:** A pick. There is no recommended winner here. The deep-dive doc already picks gameplay shape; this one picks **vibe**, and that's your call.
>
> **How to read it:** Skim the tier overview first, then read the concepts that pull you. The Comparison Matrix near the bottom is the cheat sheet. The Mix-and-Match section is for when two concepts each have something you want.

## 1. The Tiers, Briefly

| Tier | Vibe | Concepts in this doc |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Realistic / grounded** | Recognizable real-world settings, light stylization. Could be filmed. | C2 (The Circuit), C3 (Route 66) |
| **2 — Stylized real-world** | Clear real-world inspiration but with creative liberty in scale, palette, density. | C1 (Frank's Fairground), C8 (Neon Ramen Alley) |
| **3 — Whimsical / anthropomorphic** | Food has personality, the world is exaggerated, cartoon physics implied. | C5 (Counter Kingdom), C6 (Snackopolis) |
| **4 — Surreal / mythic** | Rules of reality bend. Time stretches. Worlds float. Foods become rituals. | C7 (Pantheon), C9 (Feast Through Time), C10 (Melting World) |
| **5 — Pure fantasy / abstract** | Edible cosmology. The map is the food. Food is the religion. Reality is optional. | C4 (City of Bread), C11 (Brine Deep), C12 (The Last Bite — fantasy-flavored apocalypse) |

The tiers are loose, not strict. A concept like "the map IS food" can be played as wholesome candyland (Tier 5 cozy) or as eldritch food-flesh body horror (Tier 5 dark). The profiles below pin each to a specific take.

## Concept 1 — Frank's Fairground (Coney Island Belle-Époque)

**One-line pitch:** *The Mustard Belt, the boardwalk, the Wonder Wheel — eat for glory.*

**Reality tier:** 2 (stylized real-world)

**Visual identity:**
- A turn-of-the-century boardwalk crowded with ornate towers, minarets, painted plaster columns, striped canopies, marquee lights spelling out ride names
- Wooden boardwalk planks stretching east-west along a sand beach, ocean to the south, sky-tower silhouettes on the horizon
- Bunting strung between every ride. Hand-painted ride signage. Hand-cranked organ music drifting under crowd murmur
- Palette: burgundy, ivory, antique gold, navy. Accent reds and turquoise. Everything slightly faded, like a colored postcard
- Lighting: warm low sun at "perpetual 6pm." Filament bulbs strung across the midway. Sky always feels like the end of a summer day
- Architectural language inspired by the original [1903 Luna Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luna_Park_(Coney_Island,_1903)) — Thompson's wild jumble of Middle-Eastern minarets, Romanesque arches, and Art Nouveau curves, lit by 250,000 incandescent bulbs

**Core gameplay framing:**
- Contests are the centerpiece of the boardwalk — the eating arena is literally Frank's Famous storefront, the most ornate building on the map
- Each contest is staged like a Coney Island prizefight: a barker introduces the eaters, the crowd presses against velvet rope, a brass band plays during the countdown
- The eating contest's "voice" is George Shea's absurdist oratory — already in the GAME_DESIGN doc and a perfect fit for this concept's belle-époque verbosity
- Side activities: carnival mini-games scattered between arenas (already built), rides as wallpaper, prize booths, fortune-tellers, a "freak show" tent that's actually the cosmetics shop

**Sample ride re-themes** (already-built — what they ARE in this world, not what they become):
- Carousel → A genuine 1903-style carousel with hand-carved horses, gold-leaf trim, a Wurlitzer organ
- PirateShip → "The Lusty Mermaid" pirate galleon with a barker shouting at riders to scream louder
- BigWheel → "The Great Wheel" — wood gondolas, painted ironwork, viewable from anywhere on the map
- DropTower → "The Sky Tower" — a Parachute Jump silhouette, red lattice steel, the skyline anchor

**NPC cast vibe:** Period-flavored carnies and contest entrants. Mustache wax, straw boater hats, sequined leotards.
- *Fitzwilliam "Whiskers" Garibaldi* — the mustachioed barker, hawks every contest, never lowers his voice
- *Madame Petite* — tiny fortune-teller who whispers ominous predictions before each contest
- *The Glutton King of Brooklyn* — fictional historical eating champion, has his portrait above the bar, occasionally NPC-spawns to defend his record
- *Penny Lou Sweet* — bake-sale champion turned pro eater, all aprons and grit

**Monetization angle specific to this concept:**
- Sepia-tone "vintage" cosmetic skin packs
- Painted-portrait victory pose (the screen briefly becomes a circus poster)
- Hand-painted bunting / banner emotes that hang behind your player
- Period outfits: straw boater + striped jacket, bustle dress, bare-knuckle boxer kit
- VIP "Box Seat" pass — your name on a brass plaque at the contest arena

**Target audience appeal:**
- Roblox demo: kid casual + adult ironic. Strong "vintage Americana" hook for older players nostalgic for old-photo-book Coney Island
- Comparable hit games in this aesthetic space: [Bee Swarm Simulator](https://romonitorstats.com/experience/1537690962/) (similar warm-nostalgia palette + collectible-driven progression), [Adopt Me](https://www.roblox.com/games/920587237/Adopt-Me) (cozy social fairground vibe), [Work at a Pizza Place](https://www.thespike.gg/roblox/best-roblox-games/og-games) (small-town wholesomeness)

**Build risk:**
- *Easy:* Rides are largely built. Boardwalk geometry is sand + plank + ocean — all primitives. Bunting + light strings are decorative. Palette already locked.
- *Hard:* Ornate Belle-Époque trim (minarets, painted columns, gold-leaf cornices) is hard to do with primitives alone — risk of "blocky beach" feel. Skilled use of Neon parts + texture decals will be required.
- *Approximate weeks vs current plan:* This IS the current plan. Zero additional weeks.
- *Critical technical risk:* The fairground can read as "generic carnival" rather than specifically Belle-Époque unless distinct architectural language is hit — moodboard discipline is everything

**Memorability score:** Moderate. "Old-timey carnival eating game" — easy to describe, not visually electrifying. Strong identity, weak hook. Players remember the boardwalk feel, not a single iconic image.

**One sketch paragraph:** You spawn on a wooden boardwalk that creaks softly under your character's feet. The sun is permanently low and warm. Down the strip, a ferris wheel spins in slow circles; further still, a red-lattice sky tower looms against a peach sky. Bunting flaps gently. A man in a striped jacket leans into a megaphone and yells *"STEP RIGHT UP — the Mustard Belt awaits!"* You walk past a ring-toss stall, a fortune-teller's tent, and a painted sign reading **FRANK'S FAMOUS — TODAY'S CONTEST: HOT DOGS, 4PM**. The arena door is propped open. Inside, the crowd is already murmuring. Somewhere a brass band starts up. This is going to feel like 1903.

## Concept 2 — The Circuit (Nathan's-style Hyper-Real)

**One-line pitch:** *Real arenas. Real broadcasts. The sport of eating, played straight.*

**Reality tier:** 1 (realistic / grounded)

**Visual identity:**
- Real-feeling arenas modeled on actual venues — Coney Island boardwalk, a Vegas casino floor, a Tokyo gymnasium, the Buffalo Wing Festival main stage
- Modern broadcast aesthetic: jumbotron screens, sponsor banners (fictional brands), ESPN-style chyrons and lower-thirds, a green-room behind each arena
- Modular hub: the player's "home gym" — a wall of mustard belts, a heavy bag for jaw training, a stomach-stretching chamber, a kitchen sponsor wall
- Palette: industrial steel grey, scoreboard amber, hot-dog red, jumbotron blue. Stage lights are harsh and white
- Architectural language: real-event broadcast staging — Truss, cable runs, camera dollies, EMT tent backstage
- Lighting: arena spot, harsh and unforgiving. No magic. No dust motes. Just sports.

**Core gameplay framing:**
- Contests are presented as **televised events**. The HUD frames the contest with broadcast chyrons. Tier 4 ranks feel like an ESPN main card.
- The drama is the **rivalry**: each NPC is an opponent with a career record, a head-to-head against you, and a real grudge arc
- Side activities replace carnival mini-games with **training mini-games**: water-loading challenges, jaw-press, gum-chew rep counter. They earn coins and unlock micro-stat bonuses.
- Rides are mostly absent from this concept (rides break the realism). Replace ride zones with training facilities, sponsor lounges, and a press-room interview NPC

**Sample ride re-themes:**
- Carousel → Cut, or repurposed as a "warmup spin" stat-boost station in the gym
- PirateShip → Cut, or replaced with a balance-trainer rig
- BigWheel → Sponsor-branded TV tower (decorative skyline only)
- ScreamerCoaster → Cut. Doesn't fit the realism.
- TeacupRide → Hot tea / hot soup speed-eating training booth

**NPC cast vibe:** Real-eater archetypes already in GAME_DESIGN.md — The Jaws, The Tsunami, The Notorious M.I.K.I., etc. This concept reads them straight, not parodically.
- *The Jaws* — silent menace in American flag gear, terrifying focus
- *The Tsunami* — small frame, bows before each contest, leaves a haiku on the table
- *The Coach* — your own NPC trainer, real-talk advice between contests, never absurdist
- *The Press Reporter* — interviews you after every win, headlines roll on the jumbotron

**Monetization angle specific to this concept:**
- Sponsor-jersey skins (fictional brands — "Big Hank's Beans," "Roar Energy")
- Broadcast cosmetic — your face on a jumbotron during your eating animation
- "Press kit" emotes (interview mic, signature pose)
- VIP green-room access (social space, no power)
- Real-belt cosmetic tiers (bronze, silver, gold, diamond, championship)

**Target audience appeal:**
- Roblox demo: teen flex + adult ironic. The sport-sim seriousness reads as cool to teens, as comedy to adults who recognize the genre conventions
- Comparable hit games in this aesthetic space: [Blade Ball](https://games.gg/roblox/guides/roblox-blade-ball-codes-april-2026/) (skill-PvP, sports-tournament structure), [Basketball Legends / Hoopz](https://studiokrew.com/blog/top-games-on-roblox-and-analysis-2026/) (real-sport simulator on Roblox), [WWE / wrestling sim Roblox games](https://www.switchbladegaming.com/roblox/best-games-2026/)

**Build risk:**
- *Easy:* No fantasy assets to invent. Steel-and-glass architecture is fast in Roblox primitives. Broadcast UI is just well-laid-out 2D.
- *Hard:* Realistic crowd density at scale is expensive (1,000s of NPCs). Real venues are *visually quiet* — risk of looking boring on a thumbnail.
- *Approximate weeks vs current plan:* +2 weeks (cut ride scripting, build training facilities, add broadcast HUD layer)
- *Critical technical risk:* **Thumbnails do not pop.** Roblox lives and dies on the storefront thumbnail. A boring grey arena loses to a candyland thumbnail every time.

**Memorability score:** Lower. "Realistic competitive eating sim" is a hard pitch. Strong for niche players who watch Nathan's; weak for impulse-clickers. Memorable only after they play.

**One sketch paragraph:** You spawn in your home gym. A heavy bag swings idly in the corner. A wall of belts — bronze, silver, gold — catches the fluorescent light. On a small TV, ESPN replays your last win, ticker reading **CHESTNUT-CLASS UPSET IN BUFFALO**. Your coach NPC nods from the bench: *"Big card tonight. Hot dogs. The Tsunami's in town."* You walk through a hallway lined with sponsor banners and emerge into a packed arena, crowd already roaring, jumbotron showing your face at 40 feet tall. There's no magic here. There is only the table, the timer, and the man across from you who has not lost in three years.

## Concept 3 — Route 66 Glutton Run (Diner America)

**One-line pitch:** *Cross America one diner at a time. Eat your way home.*

**Reality tier:** 1–2 (grounded with light stylization)

**Visual identity:**
- A road trip across the American South-West: Tier 1 is a small-town Kansas diner, Tier 2 is a Texas drive-in, Tier 3 is a New Mexico truck-stop, Tier 4 is a giant Vegas casino buffet
- Neon signs everywhere — pink flamingos, "EAT" in 8-foot letters, blinking arrows, vintage motel marquees
- Checkerboard floors, red vinyl booths, chrome counter stools, jukeboxes that actually play music when you tap them
- A drivable hub: the player owns a beat-up car they upgrade alongside their stats. The car carries them between tiers visually (cutscene drive between arenas).
- Palette: turquoise, neon pink, chrome silver, cherry-red leather, neon yellow. Sky always sunset-orange or midnight-purple
- Architectural language: roadside Americana — steeply pitched roofs, big windows, neon halos, exposed brick, hand-painted signs

**Core gameplay framing:**
- Each tier is a stop on a road trip. You don't fast-travel between arenas — you drive there, briefly.
- The diner is a hangout: jukebox, shake counter, claw machine (a mini-game), booths to sit in with friends
- Contests are framed as "Trucker Showdowns," "Diner Championships," "Buffet Bowls" — Americana naming for everything
- Side activities replace some carnival mini-games with jukebox-rhythm bites, milkshake-chug timer, claw machine, diner counter trivia

**Sample ride re-themes:**
- Carousel → Drive-in movie carousel (cars rotate slowly around a screen)
- PirateShip → "The Big Burger" (a giant burger that rocks like a pirate ship — the only concession to whimsy)
- BigWheel → A roadside Ferris wheel attached to a county fair scene at Tier 2
- ScreamerCoaster → "Route 66" wooden coaster at Tier 4 Vegas — still a coaster, but a Vegas-themed one
- BumperCars → Drive-in classic, fits perfectly

**NPC cast vibe:** Working-class diner regulars and traveling road-warriors
- *Dottie* — the lifer waitress at the Kansas diner. Calls you "hon." Knows everyone's order.
- *Big Earl* — trucker NPC, eats like a man trying to make a delivery deadline. Wears a cap that says JESUS LOVES YOU.
- *The King* — Elvis-impersonator eating champion. Sings before every contest. Wins more often than is reasonable.
- *Veronica Diamond* — Vegas buffet queen, sequined gown, only fights at Tier 4

**Monetization angle specific to this concept:**
- Vintage car cosmetics (you drive between arenas — the car is highly visible flex)
- Jukebox song packs (your jukebox plays during your eating contests)
- Neon-sign emote (your username glows in a neon halo above your head)
- Letterman jacket + roller-skate cosmetics
- "Vegas night" VIP pass — your tier-4 arena is platinum-themed
- Booth ownership: your name on a permanent reserved booth at the Tier 1 diner (visible to other players)

**Target audience appeal:**
- Roblox demo: teen flex + tween creative. Strong for the cottagecore-adjacent demo. Has a visual identity teens have learned to love through TikTok aesthetics (50s diner, neon, soft pink, roller-skate aesthetic)
- Comparable hit games in this aesthetic space: [Retro Diner](https://retro-diner.fandom.com/wiki/Retro_Diner) (direct genre-mate, multiplayer 50s diner sim, launched 2025), [Welcome to Bloxburg](https://romonitorstats.com/experience/15101393044/monetization/) (cozy home-life sim), [Dress to Impress](https://www.roblox.com/games/15101393044/Dress-To-Impress) (this concept gives DTI players a world to flex in)

**Build risk:**
- *Easy:* Neon parts are a Roblox native. Diner geometry is rectangle-heavy and fast to build. Color palette is forgiving.
- *Hard:* The "drive between tiers" interstitial is a custom system. Realistic car physics is a tar pit (don't do it — use cutscene).
- *Approximate weeks vs current plan:* +3 weeks (rebuild map as 4 distinct diner zones, add jukebox + car cosmetic system)
- *Critical technical risk:* Map fragmentation. Splitting one boardwalk into 4 road-trip zones means more travel friction. Mitigate with seamless walkable connections OR cutscene transitions.

**Memorability score:** High. "Roblox game where you eat your way across America from a chrome diner to a Vegas buffet" is a one-sentence pitch with image hooks. Players will remember the neon and the road.

**One sketch paragraph:** You spawn outside a roadside diner under a pink neon sign reading **DOTTIE'S**. Crickets chirp. The sky is sunset-orange. Inside, a jukebox plays Elvis. A truck rumbles past on the highway behind you. Dottie waves from behind the counter: *"Hon, the Pancake Showdown starts in five. You hungry?"* On a chalkboard above the register: **TONIGHT: PANCAKES. WINNER: $60.** Out the back window, your car is parked, hood up, waiting for you to upgrade it. Down the road, a tiny dot of neon marks the next town. You sit down at the counter, the stool creaks, and someone slides a stack of pancakes in front of you that is taller than your character. The clock above the counter ticks down. *Let's eat.*

## Concept 4 — The City of Bread (The Map IS Food)

**One-line pitch:** *The buildings are bread. The river is gravy. The contest is everywhere.*

**Reality tier:** 5 (pure fantasy / abstract — edible cosmology)

**Visual identity:**
- The map is literally made of food, at architectural scale
- A skyline of skyscraper-sized donuts, leaning like the Tower of Pisa. A river of running chocolate. Bridges of pretzels. Sidewalks of waffle. A mountain in the distance is a meatball, gently steaming. The volcano you can see from anywhere is a chocolate volcano dripping fudge lava.
- Districts: the Donut District (giant donuts as buildings), the Noodle Quarter (pasta as bridges, ramen bowls as plazas), the Cake Heights (towering frosted layer cakes), the Soup Sea (a literal lake of bisque you can swim in)
- Palette: butter-yellow, glaze-pink, chocolate-brown, cherry-red, strawberry-cream. Saturated and dessert-bright. Think [Candyland board game maps](https://acid-escape-rblx.fandom.com/wiki/Candy_Land) — chocolate rivers, marshmallow platforms, popsicle trees, waffle cottages — at city scale.
- Architectural language: leaning, soft, organic. Nothing has a clean right angle. Buildings sag like souffle. Sidewalks ripple.
- Lighting: perpetual "magic hour." Warm golden glow. Steam rising from buildings (because the food is fresh).

**Core gameplay framing:**
- The contest is not separate from the world — it's the **central ritual of the city**. The Mustard Belt is the city's crown. The reigning eater rules a district.
- Arenas are landmarks: the Tier 4 arena is *inside* the steaming meatball mountain
- Side activities are scattered through the food districts. Ride the Spaghetti River raft. Skate the Waffle Plaza. Climb the Croissant Cliffs.
- The food you eat at contests is sourced from the *map itself*: the hot dogs at the Donut District contest are harvested from a hot dog farm visible at the edge of the map. World and gameplay are unified.

**Sample ride re-themes:**
- Carousel → Cupcake Carousel — riders sit in giant cupcakes that spin
- PirateShip → A pirate ship made of crusty french bread, swings over a gravy moat
- BigWheel → A donut-shaped ferris wheel where the gondolas are bagels
- ScreamerCoaster → Licorice-rope coaster that loops through the Cake Heights
- LogFlume → Chocolate Falls — splashdown into a real chocolate pool
- TeacupRide → Genuine giant teacups, full of (decorative) tea you can splash in

**NPC cast vibe:** Citizens of food-city. Mostly humanoid, but occasionally a bread loaf walks past with arms.
- *Mayor Maple* — anthropomorphic donut, runs the city, kicks off every Tier-4 contest with a speech
- *Captain Croissant* — flaky, dramatic, captains the bread ship
- *Madame Marmalade* — runs the cosmetics shop out of a giant jam jar
- *The Sourdough Sage* — old wise bread loaf who hands out daily quests in the Bakery Quarter

**Monetization angle specific to this concept:**
- Edible cosmetic skins (be a walking ice cream cone, a sentient taco, a hot dog with eyes)
- Architectural pet companions (a tiny donut that floats next to your head)
- "Flavor Auras" — your character emits a visible perfume cloud (strawberry, garlic, vanilla)
- VIP — your home base is a tiny edible house in the Bakery Quarter, visible on the map
- Seasonal swaps: in October, the City of Bread becomes the City of Pumpkin Pie. In December, gingerbread. **Built-in content cadence by literal map skin.**

**Target audience appeal:**
- Roblox demo: kid casual (huge — kids love candyland-scale fantasy) + tween creative. This is the **strongest thumbnail concept in the menu** — a screenshot is self-explanatory and instantly viral
- Comparable hit games in this aesthetic space: [Grow a Garden](https://egw.news/gaming/news/28040/11-million-players-because-bee-swarms-the-grow-a-g-Euh7VraZR) (cozy, candy-bright, hit 11M concurrent), [Pet Simulator 99](https://www.roblox.com/games/8737899170/Pet-Simulator-99) (bright colors + cute landscape = mass appeal), [Tower of Hell](https://studiokrew.com/blog/top-games-on-roblox-and-analysis-2026/) (whimsical scale, brain-off appeal)

**Build risk:**
- *Easy:* Roblox primitives natively support "giant cylindrical donut" — Cylinder + scale, slap a sprinkle decal on top. Color is solid colors. Cylinder + Sphere + Wedge gets you 80% of food shapes.
- *Hard:* **The map is the whole game.** Every visible object needs custom modeling. Risk of "blob park" cheapness without art discipline. Soft organic shapes are harder than rectangular ones in Roblox primitives.
- *Approximate weeks vs current plan:* +6–8 weeks (entire map rebuild). But many existing rides re-skin gracefully (cupcake carousel = recolor carousel).
- *Critical technical risk:* Player traversal. A meatball mountain looks great on a thumbnail but is a navmesh nightmare. Plan walkable paths from day 1.

**Memorability score:** **Maximum.** "Roblox game where the city is made of food and you eat in it" is the single best pitch in this entire menu for one-sentence virality. A friend can describe it in 8 words.

**One sketch paragraph:** You spawn on a sidewalk shaped like a waffle. A river of melted chocolate runs past, steam curling off its surface. Across the river, a city of skyscraper-tall donuts leans toward you, lights glowing softly in their holes. Behind the donut skyline, a meatball mountain rises into a buttery-yellow sky, and beyond it, a chocolate volcano gently drips fudge lava. A walking croissant in a sailor hat tips its bicorn at you: *"Welcome to the Bakery Quarter. The Belt Match starts at the Meatball at sundown."* You can see the meatball from here. It's still steaming. Your character takes a step and the waffle sidewalk gives slightly under your foot. Somewhere, a bagel ferris wheel is turning. You have never wanted to eat a city more.

## Concept 5 — Counter Kingdom (Tiny World)

**One-line pitch:** *You're the size of an ant. The kitchen is the world.*

**Reality tier:** 3 (whimsical / anthropomorphic — bug's-eye realism)

**Visual identity:**
- Everything is enormous because the player is tiny. A cereal box is a skyscraper. The fridge is a glacier. A spilled glass of juice is a lake.
- Map = a single suburban kitchen at miniature scale: countertop, sink, stove, fridge, breakfast nook, pantry, cookie jar on the high shelf (Tier 4 arena)
- Scale relationships are extreme: a single grain of rice is the size of your character's torso. A pretzel is a piece of architecture.
- Palette: warm "morning kitchen" — pastel yellows, robin's-egg blue cabinets, white tile, marble veining. Crumbs on the counter are landmark mountains.
- Architectural language: real kitchen objects treated as ruins, towers, bridges, vehicles. The pencil cup is a town hall. The cutting board is the colosseum.
- Lighting: warm "morning sunbeam through the kitchen window." Strong shafts of light cutting across the counter make natural arena spotlights.

**Core gameplay framing:**
- The eating contests are turf wars between rival ant clans (or mouse clans, or borrower-style tiny-humans). The food is the spoils of war.
- Contests are *staged on a plate* — the contest table is the plate, the arena is the rim, spectators stand on the surrounding placemat
- Side activities are micro-explorations: climb the cookie jar, raft across a juice spill, ride a fork down a slide of mashed potato
- Tier progression = climbing higher. Tier 1 is the floor; Tier 4 is the **top shelf**.

**Sample ride re-themes:**
- Carousel → A spinning lazy-susan that's been re-skinned as a ride
- PirateShip → A measuring-spoon swing — a real measuring spoon, rocking
- BigWheel → A salad spinner with bowls as gondolas
- ScreamerCoaster → Built out of bent wire and discarded coffee straws
- LogFlume → A real-water syrup flume through a stack of pancakes

**NPC cast vibe:** Ant-folk, mouse-folk, borrower-style tiny humans. Industrious, miniature, dramatic.
- *Captain Pip* — head ant of the Counter Clan, wears a thimble as a helmet
- *Old Marble* — a wise marble that's been on the counter for "longer than anyone remembers" (visual gag: it's literally just a marble that occasionally speaks)
- *The Cookie Crumb Gang* — rival mouse clan from the pantry
- *Gigi the Roomba* — a Roomba on the floor below, mythological terror, "do not let it see you"

**Monetization angle specific to this concept:**
- Tiny-house cosmetic (live in a thimble, a teacup, a matchbox)
- "Tools" as weapons/cosmetics (toothpick sword, paperclip armor, twist-tie whip)
- Pet companions are actual bugs (a beetle, a ladybug, a moth)
- VIP unlocks the "high shelf" — a top-down view of the whole kitchen, social hangout space
- Seasonal: holiday kitchen swaps (Halloween = pumpkin spice latte on the counter, Christmas = gingerbread house at the breakfast nook)

**Target audience appeal:**
- Roblox demo: kid casual (huge — the scale is inherently funny to children) + tween creative. The "borrower" / "secret world hidden in plain sight" fantasy is potent.
- Comparable hit games in this aesthetic space: [Bee Swarm Simulator](https://romonitorstats.com/experience/1537690962/) (player is tiny in a garden — same scale fantasy), Honey-I-Shrunk-the-Kids-style games on PC (the scale fantasy [has a long history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honey,_I_Shrunk_the_Kids)), [Pikmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honey,_I_Shrunk_the_Kids) (tiny captain commanding tiny creatures in a familiar-yet-giant world)

**Build risk:**
- *Easy:* The map is *one room*. Tight scope. Cereal boxes are rectangular prisms. A toaster is two boxes with detail decals. Texture packs do most of the heavy lifting.
- *Hard:* Scale must FEEL extreme — too-small camera FOV and the magic dies. Asset library entirely new (no existing carnival props translate).
- *Approximate weeks vs current plan:* +5–6 weeks (full map rebuild, but smaller surface area than Belle-Époque map)

## Concept 12 — The Last Bite (Post-Apocalyptic Scavenger)

**One-line pitch:** *World ended. Food is rare. Eat to remember what it was.*

**Reality tier:** 4–5 (post-apocalyptic fantasy)

**Visual identity:**
- A ruined world after some food-related apocalypse. The remaining food sources are rare, mutated, and prize-worthy.
- Hub: a ramshackle frontier town built into the ruins of a giant collapsed food court. Burned-out fast-food signs lean over a dusty market. A neon arrow blinks erratically: **STILL EATING**.
- Mutated mega-foods roam the wasteland: a 50-foot pizza-slime that eats trucks, a herd of feral hot dogs, a sentient burger-tank
- Palette: rust, dust, ash, faded neon, deep maroon, mute yellow. Sunsets are blood-orange. The sky is hazy.
- Architectural language: ruins + makeshift. Corrugated steel patched over melted McDonald's arches. Cardboard tents. Junkyard chic.
- Inspired by [Apocalypse Rising 2's ruined-world aesthetic](https://www.roblox.com/games/863266079/Apocalypse-Rising-2), proven Roblox audience

**Core gameplay framing:**
- The contest is a **survivor tournament**. The Mustard Belt has been lost since the apocalypse. The reigning champion gets to eat **last** — the highest honor in a hungry world.
- Side activities: scavenge mutated food, hunt feral hot dog packs, fortify your camp
- Tier progression = expeditions to more dangerous regions. Tier 4 is the **Glowing Buffet** — the irradiated remains of a Las Vegas casino buffet, perpetually serving radioactive food. Champions only.
- Tone is **wry comedy in a serious wrapper** — apocalypse is the joke; the seriousness with which characters treat eating is the punchline

**Sample ride re-themes:**
- Carousel → A rusted carousel that still creaks to life when activated, horses missing
- PirateShip → A pirate ship cobbled out of fast-food signage and shopping carts
- BigWheel → A ferris wheel turned makeshift watchtower
- ScreamerCoaster → A coaster ride straight into a feral burger nest — full of risk + reward
- BumperCars → Bumper-cars-as-actual-vehicles for raiding mutated food caravans

**NPC cast vibe:** Dusty post-apocalyptic survivors with weirdly specific food fixations
- *Sheriff Crust* — runs the frontier town, lost his family to a pizza-slime, hates the slime
- *Bone Daddy* — a fry cook turned warlord, still wears his apron
- *The Glowing Lady* — an irradiated diner waitress who works the Tier 4 buffet, immune to everything
- *Old Man Krinkle* — toothless wanderer who remembers what bread used to taste like, gives lore quests

**Monetization angle specific to this concept:**
- Post-apocalyptic cosmetic skins (gas masks, leather armor, scavenged outfits)
- Junk pets (a tiny mutated baby pizza, a one-eyed hot dog)
- Camp customization cosmetics (decorate your scavenger tent)
- VIP — pre-apocalypse cosmetics (you get to dress like the world used to look)
- Limited "found vinyl record" emotes — your contests play scratched pre-apocalypse music

**Target audience appeal:**
- Roblox demo: teen flex (post-apoc is teen-coded — see Apocalypse Rising 2 success), some adult ironic. **Lower kid-casual appeal** — wasteland aesthetics are a barrier for the youngest players.
- Comparable hit games in this aesthetic space: [Apocalypse Rising 2](https://www.roblox.com/games/863266079/Apocalypse-Rising-2) (proven teen audience for ruined-world Roblox), [Survival: Apocalypse](https://roblox.fandom.com/wiki/Community:Garnold/Survival:_Apocalypse) (zombie wasteland), [Dead Mist 2](https://www.robloxgo.com/list/best_roblox_apocalypse_games) (open-world post-apocalyptic)

**Build risk:**
- *Easy:* Ruins look better the rougher they are — perfect for primitive-block construction. Dust + fog hide quality issues. Rusted texture decals carry a lot of weight.
- *Hard:* Selling apocalypse without going too dark for younger Roblox curation. Need to keep tone wry-not-grim.
- *Approximate weeks vs current plan:* +5–6 weeks (full map rebuild, new mutated-food asset library, new NPC voice/tone direction)
- *Critical technical risk:* **Roblox curation algorithm bias against "dark" thumbnails.** Apocalypse aesthetics can be down-ranked by the platform. Counter with bright color accents in marketing.

**Memorability score:** High. "Post-apocalyptic eating tournament where bread is sacred" is striking. The image of a 50-foot mutated pizza eating a truck is shareable.

**One sketch paragraph:** You spawn in a dust-blown frontier town built into the bones of a collapsed food court. A faded sign over the gate reads **MAULSVILLE — POP. 47 — STILL EATING**. A neon arrow on a broken pole points toward a half-buried sign that used to say something about pizza. Sheriff Crust leans against a corrugated tin wall, chewing on a stale crust: *"You here for the tournament? Champion eats first. Everyone else eats what's left."* In the distance, beyond the town's barricades, a low rumble shakes the ground — a feral pizza-slime is rolling across the wasteland, eating a school bus. The sun is setting blood-orange behind the ruins of a giant McDonald's arch. Inside the town's diner — somehow still functioning — the Tier 1 contest is starting. The food on the table is genuinely rare. Everyone in the town is watching, hungry. You take your seat. The fork is real metal. It's the most valuable thing here.

## Mix-and-Match Guidance

Most of these concepts have at least one neighbor they blend cleanly with. Some interesting pairings:

**Frank's Fairground + The City of Bread (C1 + C4).** Frank's Fairground sits on a giant hot dog bun. The boardwalk's terrain is a bun, the ocean is mustard, the sky tower is a corn dog stuck in the bun. You preserve every ride you've built AND you get the most viral thumbnail concept in the menu. **This is a very promising mix.**

**The Circuit + Route 66 (C2 + C3).** Realistic competitive eating circuit, where each tour stop is a real-feeling diner-town in America. Keeps the sport-sim drama but gives it a road-trip texture and softens the bleak realism with Americana warmth.

**Snackopolis + Counter Kingdom (C6 + C5).** A city of anthropomorphic food, but the player is also ant-sized — so the talking hot dogs are giants compared to you. The eaten-by-the-tiny-human gag becomes a literal scale gag. Pixar-quality concept.

**Cyberpunk + Pantheon (C8 + C7).** Megacorp food deities. The Burger Goddess is a holographic CEO. Worshippers wear LED-laurel-crowns. Modern myth.

**Time Travel + Mythology (C9 + C7).** Time travel where each era's tier is a different mythology's food gods — Greek pantheon (Tier 1), Norse feast halls (Tier 2), Mesoamerican chocolate gods (Tier 3), modern American food worship (Tier 4 — the Burger Goddess is the latest god).

**Belle-Époque + Surreal (C1 + C10).** Coney Island, but the rides are slightly off. The carousel horses are melting. The mirrors in the funhouse are too truthful. Plays well as a horror-light Halloween skin of the existing fairground.

**Underwater + The Map IS Food (C11 + C4).** The map is a giant tureen of soup that you're inside. Everything edible. The kelp is noodles. The fish are dumplings. The ground is rice.

**Apocalypse + Cyberpunk (C12 + C8).** Cyberpunk ruined. The neon still flickers but the corp-sponsored food courts are abandoned. Roving gangs of holographic-ad-haunted scavengers. Edgy teen-flex maximum.

**Rule of thumb for blending:** Pick two concepts that share at least ONE tier — easier than crossing tier boundaries. Belle-Époque (tier 2) + Map IS Food (tier 5) works because the gap is bridged by playful palette overlap. Pantheon (tier 4) + The Circuit (tier 1) is harder — too tonally opposed.

## What Stays Consistent Regardless of Concept

These elements are concept-independent. You can pick any concept above without redesigning them. They're the foundation under any visual direction.

**Gameplay foundation (from GAME_DESIGN.md and the deep-dive doc):**
- The four-stat upgrade system (Stomach Capacity, Jaw Speed, Swallow Rate, Focus). Names may flavor differently (in Cyberpunk they could be "Throughput / Bandwidth / Buffer / Override"; in Pantheon they could be "Belly of Bakar / Jaws of Khepri / Throat of Maw / Eye of Hephaestomach"), but the underlying math is identical.
- The 90-second contest core loop with skill-check minigames per food type
- The Reversal of Fortune fill-meter-of-doom mechanic
- The 4-player table with NPC backfill (the "Bee Swarm hybrid" PvE-anchored model from the deep-dive doc)
- The Chipmunk window in the last 10 seconds
- The prestige belt system (re-skinned per concept — Mustard Belt, Pearl of Hunger, Chrome Belt, Chrono-Belt, Belt of Hephaestomach, etc.)
- Daily quests, weekly contest format rotation, seasonal events

**Monetization rules (from CLAUDE.md):**
- Cosmetic-only Robux. Never sell stat upgrades. Dual currency (Coins gameplay-only, Style Bucks cosmetic).
- The rarest pets are achievement-locked, not Robux-locked
- 15-minute monetization gate
- VIP is social/aesthetic, never stat-boosting

**Onboarding skeleton (from GAME_DESIGN.md section 9):**
- Spawn → walk to first arena via guided NPC → all-NPC first contest with assist → win → first upgrade prompt → world opens
- Total: under 2 minutes to first stat upgrade
- The NPC delivering this (George Shea, Dottie, Captain Pip, Doctor Tempus, etc.) is concept-flavored but functionally identical

**Social hooks:**
- Friends leaderboard
- Spectator throw-food + cheer mechanics
- Visible prestige auras
- Public records / champion titles

**Technical foundation:**
- Roblox primitives + Neon parts as the visual language. NO external mesh pipelines for standard assets (per art-style memory feedback).
- Rojo for sync. Server-authoritative for stats. Client-side for UI/VFX only.
- The mini-game stall visual contract (CameraTarget + CameraOrigin parts) from CLAUDE.md — works in every concept

**NPC cast structure (from GAME_DESIGN.md):**
- Every NPC has one special move, one personality hook, one visual signature
- The Bear archetype as P5 endgame (re-themed per concept: The Bear → The God → The Slime King → The Iron Chef → etc.)

**The "what makes this game different" pillars:**
- Reversal-of-Fortune-as-feature (not in any other Roblox eating game)
- George Shea-style absurdist announcer voice (just renamed per concept)
- Named NPC rivals with personalities (re-cast per concept)
- Spectator-as-participant mechanics

If you commit to a concept, take this list and **don't redesign anything in it**. These have been researched, validated, and refined. The concept choice is purely about world and theme — the loop underneath is already locked in.

## Closing Note

This menu is exhaustive but not infinite. If none of the 12 concepts pull you, the most common "concept I didn't include" categories worth a one-line sketch:

- **Sports anime** (Food Wars-coded — earnest shonen drama, anime-stylized presentation, training arcs as upgrade gates)
- **Murder mystery dinner party** (Clue-coded — contests are alibis, NPCs have secret motives)
- **High fantasy / Lord of the Rings** (Hobbit-feast-coded — Shire taverns, dwarven beer halls, elven dessert courts)
- **Western frontier saloon** (Red Dead-coded — overlaps with Route 66 but with horses)
- **Steampunk Victorian** (overlaps with Belle-Époque and Time Travel — brass-and-gears eating contests in dirigibles)
- **Cyber-cute / Y2K kawaii** (Sanrio-coded — pastel cyberpunk, mascot-driven, Hello Kitty + Cuphead)

Each of these could be developed into a full profile if any of them resonates. But more concepts isn't always better — at this point Antoine should pick or blend, not browse forever. The deep-dive doc already says: **commit to a vibe and ship Tier 1 fast.** This menu is the buffet; close it when you're full.
