# FunGuy — Species Appearance Reference
*35 species, tiered. Descriptions capture real-world appearance adapted for low-poly bioluminescent cave aesthetic.*

---

## Common (12 species) — 1–1.5 studs tall, 1–2 studs wide

### Button Mushroom (`button`)
A perfectly round, smooth ivory-white cap sits atop a short, thick cylindrical stem. The cap is slightly domed when young and flattens as it matures. Gills underneath are pale pink, tightly packed. The whole mushroom has a clean, clinical look — almost too perfect. In-cave: subtle warm white glow at the cap rim.

### Oyster Mushroom (`oyster`)
Fan-shaped caps in overlapping clusters, growing sideways from a stubby off-center stem. Color ranges from pale grey to cream with a slight blue-silver sheen. The cap edges are gently ruffled and wavy. Gills run down the stem in tight, pale ridges. In-cave: soft silver-blue bioluminescent shimmer along the gill lines.

### Shiitake (`shiitake`)
A broad, umbrella-shaped brown cap with a cracked, pale tan pattern radiating from the center like a dried-earth mosaic. The edges curl slightly downward. Stem is fibrous, cream-colored, and slightly off-center. Gills are white, tightly packed, with a delicate veil remnant around the upper stem. In-cave: warm amber glow through the cracked cap pattern.

### Chanterelle (`chanterelle`)
Golden-yellow, with a wavy, irregularly shaped cap that's deeply funnel-like at the center and ruffled at the edges. Instead of gills, it has forking ridges — blunt, vein-like — that run down the stem. The whole mushroom has a smooth, apricot-gold color, consistent from cap to base. In-cave: warm golden light pulses slowly, like a tiny lantern.

### Portobello (`portobello`)
A large, flat, open cap — dark chocolate brown on top, with deep dark gills exposed underneath. The cap is wide relative to the stem, giving it a bold, architectural silhouette. Stem is thick, pale, and fibrous. The cap surface has a slightly velvety texture with faint concentric rings. In-cave: deep amber-brown glow along the gill edges.

### Enoki (`enoki`)
Long, impossibly thin white stems topped with tiny pin-sized caps, always growing in dense clusters. The stems are slightly translucent, almost glassy. The caps are a warm cream-white, smooth and round. Together they look like a field of tiny pale needles. In-cave: soft white glow runs up each stem like a fiber-optic thread.

### Maitake (`maitake`)
A large, layered mass of overlapping grey-brown fronds, each with a wavy edge, radiating from a central base like a frozen explosion. Resembles ruffled feathers or a hen's back — hence the name "Hen of the Woods" (though that's a separate species here). In-cave: dark grey fronds with silver-green bioluminescent edge lining.

### Porcini (`porcini`)
A bulbous, rounded brown cap sits atop a thick, swollen white stem covered in a fine net-like (reticulate) pattern. The underside is a sponge of tiny cream-colored pores rather than gills. The cap is smooth, slightly sticky, deep chocolate-to-tan. The stem is stocky and pale. In-cave: warm cream-gold glow from the pore layer beneath the cap.

### Morel (`morel`)
A hollow, conical cap completely covered in a deep honeycomb network of ridges and pits — like a natural lattice or sponge. Color is earthy tan to dark brown. The cap sits directly on a hollow white stem, and the entire interior is open. Its alien geometry makes it instantly recognizable. In-cave: the honeycomb pattern catches and channels pale amber light.

### Lion's Mane (`lionsmane`)
A cascading pom-pom of long, icicle-like white spines drooping downward from a central mass, with no visible cap or stem. Completely white, shaggy, and soft-looking, like a small frozen waterfall or a sea anemone. In-cave: each spine tip emits a cool blue-white bioluminescent glow, making it look like a cluster of tiny lights.

### King Trumpet (`kingtrumpet`)
A thick, meaty white cylindrical stem dominates the form — far wider than typical mushrooms — topped by a small, flat brown cap that seems almost afterthought-sized. The stem has a smooth, firm texture with subtle lengthwise fibering. The cap is pale grey-brown, slightly convex. In-cave: pale violet glow rises from the base of the stem upward.

### Cremini (`cremini`)
Nearly identical in shape to the Button Mushroom but with a light brown cap — earthy tan to caramel. Slightly firmer-looking, with gills that are pale pink aging to dark brown. The cap stays more firmly domed than a button. An unassuming, reliable presence. In-cave: soft warm orange ambient glow at the cap center.

## Uncommon (8 species) — 2–2.5 studs tall, 2–3 studs wide

### Hen of the Woods (`henofwoods`)
Massive overlapping rosettes of dark grey-brown wavy fronds, each frond tongue-shaped with a paler underside. Grows in a wide fan from a central branching base. Fronds have a slightly leathery, matte surface. From above it looks like a dark flower. In-cave: silvery-green shimmer on each frond's underside, pulsing outward from center.

### Black Trumpet (`blacktrumpet`)
A deeply funnel-shaped, trumpet-like mushroom with no true cap — the top flares open like a horn. Dark grey to near-black on the outside, paler grey inside. The surface is finely wrinkled and channeled, with no distinct gills. They grow in clusters, each an elegant hollow cone. In-cave: dark charcoal body with a faint deep violet glow rising from inside the funnel.

### Lobster Mushroom (`lobster`)
Not a single species but a parasitic mold that coats other mushrooms in a vivid cracked orange-red shell — like a lobster's carapace. The original mushroom shape underneath causes irregular lumps and bumps on the surface. The color is intense: bright orange-red to vermillion, with white flesh visible in cracks. In-cave: glowing coral-orange with thin white cracks that pulse brighter periodically.

### Coral Fungus (`coralfungus`)
Looks exactly like its namesake — branching, upward-reaching fingers of pale yellow to cream, smooth and rounded at the tips, densely packed like an undersea coral formation. Individual branches split repeatedly, creating a fractal, bushy structure. In-cave: each branch tip emits a soft yellow-green bioluminescent glow that ripples down the branches.

### Cauliflower Fungus (`cauliflower`)
Extremely dense, deeply convoluted cream-to-pale-yellow fronds folded tightly upon each other, looking almost brain-like or cloud-like. No clear cap or stem — just a mass of ruffled, wavy lobes. Grows quite large. The surface has a slightly rough, brain-coral texture. In-cave: warm golden light suffuses from within the folds, giving it a lantern-like quality.

### Chestnut Mushroom (`chestnut`)
Small, classic mushroom caps in tight clusters — each cap warm chestnut-brown on top, fading to cream at the edges, with white gills below. The caps are convex, smooth, and slightly glossy. Stems are slender and pale. They grow tightly together, caps sometimes touching. In-cave: rich amber-brown glow at the cap centers, individually subtle but striking in clusters.

### Velvet Pioppino (`velvetpiop`)
Small to medium mushrooms with dark chocolate-brown caps that have a distinctly velvety, matte texture — like dark suede. The cap is bell-shaped when young and opens flat with age, with a slight umbo (central bump). Stem is slender, pale tan, slightly fibrous. Grows in loose clusters. In-cave: deep purple-brown glow at the cap center fading to dark edges.

### Nameko (`nameko`)
Small, glossy amber-orange caps in dense clusters — each cap smooth and wet-looking due to a natural gelatinous coating. The color is a rich caramel-to-tangerine orange, very uniform and saturated. Stems are slender, pale. The glossy surface reflects light vividly. In-cave: bright warm orange glow amplified by the reflective surface; looks like a cluster of tiny glowing gems.

## Rare (7 species) — 3–3.5 studs tall, 3–4 studs wide

### Matsutake (`matsutake`)
A thick, firm mushroom with a white-to-brown mottled cap that has a fibrous, shaggy surface and a thick partial veil leaving a prominent ring on the stem. Cap ranges from white to cinnamon-brown, often staining brown with age. The stem is dense and white below the ring. Has a singular, distinctive silhouette — robust and architectural. In-cave: warm cream glow with cinnamon-brown accents; the ring on the stem pulses subtly.

### Caesar's Mushroom (`caesars`)
A dramatic Amanita: the young fruiting body emerges from a pure white egg-like sac (volva) at the base. The mature cap is vivid scarlet-orange to deep red, smooth and convex, with bright yellow-orange gills and a yellow stem inside the white volva. The color contrast — red cap, yellow stem, white base — is striking. In-cave: the cap glows bright orange-red, the volva glows soft white, creating a flame-like effect.

### Saffron Milk Cap (`saffronmilk`)
A flat to funnel-shaped orange cap with concentric faint darker rings, smooth and slightly tacky. Gills are the same vivid orange. When damaged, it "bleeds" bright saffron-orange latex drops. Stem is pale orange, hollow. The uniform warm orange throughout is distinctive. In-cave: deep saffron-amber glow throughout, with bright orange "drip" particles slowly falling from the cap edge.

### Indigo Milk Cap (`indigomilk`)
A strikingly unusual mushroom: the cap is blue-grey with concentric darker bands, smooth and waxy. Gills are deep blue. When cut, it bleeds dark indigo-blue latex — one of the most visually dramatic fungi in nature. The blue becomes blue-green with age. In-cave: deep indigo-blue bioluminescent glow with slow blue droplet particles falling from the gills.

### Chicken of the Woods (`chickenwoods`)
Broad, overlapping shelf brackets of vivid yellow-orange and bright yellow. Each bracket is smooth, wavy-edged, and intensely colored — almost unnaturally so. They grow in large layered fans on wood, stacking like plates. The top surface is orange, the underside a pale cream-yellow with tiny pores. In-cave: blazing orange-yellow glow radiating outward from the center; the brightest mushroom in the cave.

### Giant Puffball (`giantpuffball`)
A large, perfectly smooth white sphere — can be the size of a soccer ball or bigger. No cap, no stem, no gills — just a round white ball sitting on the ground. Surface is smooth and slightly leathery when young, yellowing with age. Its perfect spherical form looks almost artificial. In-cave: pure white glow, steady and strong, like a miniature moon sitting on the cave floor.

### Amanita Muscaria — Fly Agaric (`flyagaric`)
The most iconic mushroom in human culture: a vivid scarlet-red dome cap covered in irregular white warts (remnants of the universal veil). The cap is perfectly hemisphrical when young, flattening with age. A sturdy white stem with a prominent skirt-like ring (annulus) partway up, and a bulbous white base (volva) emerging from the ground like a broken eggshell. Everything about it says "danger" — the red screams warning, the white spots demand attention. In-cave: the red cap glows with deep vermillion-crimson light; the white spots blaze pure white like scattered stars on a red planet; the ring on the stem pulses faintly. One of the most recognizable silhouettes in the entire game.

### Hedgehog Mushroom (`hedgehog`)
A pale cream to pale yellow cap, convex to flat, smooth on top — unremarkable at first glance. The distinction is the underside: instead of gills, it's covered in hundreds of tiny, densely packed cream-white spines (teeth) hanging downward like a tiny forest of stalactites. In-cave: the pale cap glows softly gold; the spine tips emit pinpoints of cool blue-white light.

## Epic (5 species) — 4–4.5 studs tall, 4–5 studs wide

### Ghost Fungus (`ghostfungus`)
Flat, wavy, bracket-like caps in overlapping clusters — pale cream to white, with a slightly translucent quality. Gills are white and closely spaced. What makes it extraordinary: it genuinely bioluminescens in real life, emitting a faint green-white glow. The cap surface is smooth and almost waxy. In-cave: full green-white bioluminescent glow across the entire cap — the archetypal glowing cave mushroom.

### Bleeding Tooth (`bleedingtooth`)
A young, pure white mushroom with an irregular, lumpy, rounded cap — almost like a soft white rock. As it matures, bright blood-red droplets form across the cap surface and drip from the edges (guttation of red sap). The contrast of white and blood-red is visceral and startling. In-cave: white body with animated red droplets slowly oozing down the cap, glowing crimson.

### Amethyst Deceiver (`amethyst`)
Small but intensely colored — every part is vivid violet-purple: cap, stem, gills. The cap is convex and smooth, fading to lilac as it dries. Stem is slender, same purple hue. The color is fully saturated and uniform — there is nothing else like it in color. In-cave: deep purple bioluminescent glow covers the entire mushroom; the brightest purple thing in the cave.

### Violet Webcap (`violetweb`)
A medium-to-large Cortinarius: convex to flat lilac-violet cap with a smooth, silky surface. The distinctive feature is the cortina — fine cobweb-like violet threads connecting the cap edge to the stem when young, leaving rusty-brown remnants as spores fall. Stem is club-shaped at the base, pale violet. In-cave: violet glow with animated thread-like cobweb particles floating between cap and stem.

### Bioluminescent Panellus (`biolumpanellus`)
Small, fan-shaped orange-brown caps in overlapping clusters on a wall or log surface — similar in form to oyster mushrooms but smaller and distinctly orange-tinted. Gills are pale tan. In real life it glows green-blue in the dark — one of the brightest naturally bioluminescent fungi. In-cave: orange-brown cap with intense green-blue glow from the gills and underside; two-tone light effect.

## Mythic (3 species) — 5–7 studs tall, 5–6 studs wide

### Golden Teacher (`goldenteacher`)
A large, classic mushroom form: broad golden-caramel cap, smooth and convex with a slight central nipple, pale yellow-gold gills underneath. The stem is thick, white, with a prominent skirt-like ring (annulus) midway up. Named "Golden Teacher" for its distinctively large gold cap. In-cave: radiant golden-amber glow fills the area around it; the ring on the stem glows white like a halo, and cap edges pulse with slow golden waves.

### Azure Dragon Oyster (`azuredragon`)
A fictional mythic variant of the oyster mushroom — enormous fan-shaped clusters in deep sapphire blue with iridescent dragon-scale patterning across the cap surface. Each scale catches light differently, creating a shifting, oil-slick shimmer. The gills are silver-white. Multiple caps layer over each other like armor plates. In-cave: deep blue bioluminescent glow with shifting iridescent highlights across each scale; slow color cycling between blue and teal.

### Starfire Cap (`starfire`)
Fictional mythic. A large, perfectly circular convex cap, near-black on top with a radiating star pattern of bioluminescent white-gold veins spreading from the center to the edges — like a map of a galaxy. The stem is dark, smooth, slightly translucent. The underside of the cap glows pure white. In-cave: the star-vein pattern pulses and rotates slowly; surrounding area lit by the cap's glow; particles of light drift upward like spores escaping into space.
